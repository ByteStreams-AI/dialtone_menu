Features / Delivery
Delivery

Delivery, without handing over the customer.

A marketplace takes the order, the customer, and a cut of the food. DialTone does the opposite: the guest orders from you — by phone, from your website, or in your app — and a courier is dispatched to bring it. You keep the margin, the customer record, and the relationship.

The courier fee is quoted live against the real dropoff address and folded into the total before the guest pays, so nobody is surprised and no order is dispatched at a loss.

  • Delivery from every channel — phone, web, and app
  • On-demand couriers, no fleet to hire and no shifts to fill
  • No commission on the food — you pay for the delivery, not the order
  • The customer's name, number, and history stay in your system

It refuses the orders it should refuse.

Most delivery problems are accepted at the door. An address just outside the range, an order too small to be worth driving, a kitchen that has already stopped cooking — each one becomes a refund and an unhappy guest an hour later.

DialTone checks the address against your delivery range while the guest is still ordering, and the phone agent says so out loud rather than taking the order and failing later. A delivery minimum is enforced in the same place, on every channel, so it cannot be talked around.

  • Address range checked before the order is taken, not after payment
  • Delivery minimum enforced identically on phone, web, and app
  • Out-of-range guests are offered pickup instead of being turned away

The courier is called after the money clears.

A courier is a commitment: dispatch one and the trip is billable whether or not the guest ever pays. So DialTone quotes at checkout and dispatches only once the payment has actually gone through. An abandoned cart costs you nothing.

If the guest takes their time deciding and the quote goes stale, it is re-quoted automatically rather than failing the order.

  • Dispatch happens after payment clears, never before
  • An abandoned checkout never leaves you owing a cancellation fee
  • Stale quotes are refreshed rather than dropped

For a truck, it delivers from where you actually are.

A food truck's registered address is usually a commissary it is nowhere near. Measuring delivery from there accepts guests the truck cannot reach and refuses ones standing a block away — and every refusal looks perfectly reasonable, which is why it goes unnoticed.

DialTone measures from the stop you are working, and sends the courier to the truck rather than to the paperwork.

  • Range measured from today's stop, not the registered address
  • The courier is dispatched to where the food actually is
  • Move to a new lot and delivery follows you
