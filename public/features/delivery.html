Delivery

Two couriers bid. You keep the difference.

When a delivery order comes in, DialTone asks every courier network you're connected to what it would charge and how fast it would be — then dispatches the one that wins. You are not locked to a single carrier's price on a Friday night, and you are not paying a marketplace a percentage of an order that was already yours.

DoorDash Drive and Uber Direct are courier networks, not marketplaces. The guest orders from you, on your website or over the phone; the courier only carries the bag. Your customer, your menu, your prices, your Stripe account.

  • Every delivery quoted across your connected carriers
  • Winner chosen on availability, price, and speed
  • Pin one carrier instead, if you have a rate you like
  • No commission on the order — you pay the delivery fee, not a cut

Availability decides who is even in the running.

Both carriers are asked at the same time, and neither one's bad night costs you the order. A network that doesn't cover the address, has no courier free, or simply doesn't answer is out of the running — the other one still delivers. Asking two is exactly what makes one failing survivable.

  • Carriers quoted in parallel, not one after the other
  • Out of range or no courier available means that carrier is skipped
  • A carrier timing out never blocks or cancels the order
  • Losing quotes cost nothing — a courier is only committed on payment

Cheapest wins — unless speed is worth more to you.

Out of the box the lowest quote takes the delivery, because that needs no explanation and can't surprise you. If a faster courier is worth paying for, you set one number: what a minute is worth to your restaurant. Value a minute at 25¢ and a carrier arriving twenty minutes sooner wins whenever it costs less than five dollars more.

One number, not a rules engine. "How much is a minute worth to you?" is a question you can answer on a phone call; a weighting policy is not.

  • Default: the cheapest quote wins, every time
  • Raise the minute value to let a faster carrier outbid a cheaper one
  • The same rule runs on phone, web, app, and counter orders

Or pick your carrier and stay with it.

Negotiated a rate you like? Had a run of bad handoffs from one network? Pin the carrier you want and only that one is asked. The pin is absolute on purpose: if your chosen carrier can't take the delivery, the delivery doesn't go out rather than quietly going out on the network you rejected. You made a decision, and the system doesn't overrule it behind your back.

  • Pin a carrier per restaurant, changeable whenever you like
  • Pinned means exclusive — no silent fallback to the other network
  • Unset it any time to go back to competitive bidding

Every quote is kept, including the losing one.

The bid that didn't win is the only honest record of what each network actually charges you, run after run, on your real addresses at your real hours. That is the number you want in front of you the next time a carrier wants to talk about rates — and it is the only way a claim about what bidding saved you can be worth anything.

  • Winning and losing quotes both recorded against the order
  • Real rate history on your own delivery radius, not a published table
  • Orders where one carrier was pinned or silent are not counted as competitions
